# DESIGN — THE HALO SYSTEM

## 1. The Trust

- Funds recovered to the people are held in a people's trust,
  administered with AI assistance and published accounts.
- **Personal allotment, calibrated:** each person receives a
  monthly amount determined by simulation — enough to support a
  life, deliberately *below* the level that would let them simply
  quit working. The amount is a found number, not a guessed one:
  it is calibrated per community against that community's costs
  and responses. At retirement, the allotment becomes the
  person's to use freely.
- **Needs first:** before and alongside personal allotments,
  funds are allotted to what the community's own mirror shows is
  needed first — the founding examples are homelessness, then
  new homes, then the next category *that place* has. Categories
  differ by city because needs differ by city.

## 2. The Mirror Network

- Each community runs a simulation of itself — a MiroFish-class
  agent simulation whose agents are calibrated per role and per
  task (see CALIBRATION.md).
- The mirror is fed two data streams:
  (a) the community's **documented history** — its own accounts
      of events, including the gaps. The gaps matter: through
      history, the first act of conquest was to burn the books
      and beliefs of the conquered, so the surviving record is
      partial *by design of the conquerors*. A mirror that knows
      its record was tampered with calibrates differently from
      one that mistakes the taught version for the whole truth.
  (b) **live community statistics** — the working numbers of the
      place (in the founding example: the data a mayor's office
      holds on what actually needs fixing).
- The mirror's job is decisions before money: allocations,
  removals, legal changes are run in simulation first; the
  community sees projected cause and effect before committing.

## 3. Counterfactuals

- The mirror runs the community's own history with single changes:
  a ruler who never acted, a book-burning that never happened, a
  law never passed, a fund never captured, an invention arriving
  earlier or later.
- Counterfactuals are not limited to politics and law. Industry
  cascades are first-class: an invention (the founding example:
  hydraulics) changes construction speed, which changes what gets
  built, which changes cities, which changes everything priced
  after it. Run forward to the industrial and information ages,
  or work backwards from the present to find the hinge.
- Every participant community is a lab: different events,
  different stages, many hands. That is why the help is needed
  from everybody — no single team can run the world's
  counterfactuals.

## 4. The Justice Layer

- Disclosure before judgement: a case's entire disclosure can be
  placed before reasonable agents that do not prolong the
  process. The design question the layer answers is how much of
  the multiplied judiciary — and the plea-bargain economy that
  feeds on delay — is load-bearing, and how much is demand
  created by the corruption itself.
- The measured expectation of the design: run fairly, the
  system's outputs invert — unlawfulness diminishes instead of
  increasing, jails empty of the people who never needed to be
  there, and the professions shrink to the size justice actually
  requires. A fair system is a smaller system.

## 5. Federation

- Nodes keep their own calibrations, beliefs and categories.
- Results roll up: city → province/state → country → worldwide —
  not as orders, but as published findings.
- Adoption is by example only: a people sees how another place
  lives, runs the mirror on it, and decides for itself whether to
  incorporate the law, the practice, or the belief. Joining is a
  choice; so is not joining.
- Above the variation, one governing rule of law binds every
  participant (GOVERNING-RULE.md). Everything else is local.

## 6. What the system is not

- Not a world government. Armies cannot cross the oceans to rule
  the world, and this design sends none: it sends ideas, which
  cross for free.
- Not a belief. It is a runnable test of one. Communities that
  run it and find it false are invited to publish that too —
  graded by the same gate.
