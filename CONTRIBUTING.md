# CONTRIBUTING TO HALO

HALO is released freely (see [LICENSE.md](LICENSE.md)). Anyone may
contribute; there is no gatekeeper and no membership. Contributions
are improvements to a public work — by contributing you release
your contribution on the same terms you received this work:
freely, no credit required, no proceeds owed.

## Ways to contribute

- **Stand up a node.** The most valuable contribution is a real
  community node: copy
  [city-template/CITY-NODE.md](city-template/CITY-NODE.md), fill
  it with your place's own public numbers, and publish your
  results (see [SIMULATION-GUIDE.md](SIMULATION-GUIDE.md)).
- **Publish simulation results.** Open an issue with the
  "Simulation result" template: your question, your assumptions,
  your data sources, your output, and your declared limitations.
- **Improve the design.** Propose edits to the framework
  documents by pull request. Every proposed change should say
  what it changes, why, and what evidence or reasoning supports
  it.
- **Translate.** Translations are contributions of the first
  rank. Keep the structure; note any passage you could not
  carry across.
- **Report problems.** Errors, unclear passages, and broken
  reasoning are worth an issue. Say where, and what fails.

## Rules of the work

- **Evidence discipline.** Distinguish, in everything you file,
  between measured results, cited sources, and hypotheses.
  Label hypotheses as hypotheses. A simulation output is a
  model's output — never present it as a proven real-world
  result.
- **Privacy.** Never commit personal, confidential, or
  sensitive data — yours or anyone else's. Community data is
  published in aggregate, with the community's consent (see
  SECURITY.md). When in doubt, leave it out and describe the
  gap instead.
- **No imposition.** Nodes adopt, adapt, or reject by their own
  people's choice. A pull request that tries to make one
  place's choices binding on another is out of scope by design.
- **Declared gaps beat invented completeness.** If the record
  is missing, say so — the template has a section for exactly
  that.

## Pull requests

Use the pull request template. Small, single-purpose changes
are reviewed fastest. Changes to GOVERNING-RULE.md carry the
heaviest bar: the governing rule is adopted by sight, never
imposed, and is not edited by pull request — discuss it in an
issue first.
